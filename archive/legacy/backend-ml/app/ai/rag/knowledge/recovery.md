# Recovery and Injury Prevention

## Sleep — The Most Important Recovery Tool

Sleep is when your body actually adapts to training. During deep sleep, growth hormone peaks, muscle protein synthesis accelerates, and the brain consolidates motor learning.

**How much you need:**
- **Adults:** 7-9 hours per night
- **Athletes / heavy training:** 8-10 hours
- **Teens:** 8-10 hours

**Effects of poor sleep:**
- Strength drops 5-15% after one bad night
- Reaction time slows
- Hunger hormones (ghrelin) increase, satiety hormones (leptin) decrease — making fat loss harder
- Testosterone drops 10-15% after a week of 5-hour nights
- Injury risk increases 1.7x

**Sleep hygiene basics:**
- Cool, dark, quiet room (60-68°F / 15-20°C)
- No screens 30-60 min before bed
- Consistent bedtime/wake time
- Avoid caffeine after 2pm
- Avoid heavy meals or alcohol within 3 hours of sleep
- Get sunlight in the morning to anchor your circadian rhythm

## Active Recovery

Light movement on rest days is better than total inactivity. It increases blood flow, reduces soreness, and aids in waste removal from muscles.

**Active recovery options:**
- Walking (30-45 minutes)
- Light cycling
- Yoga or mobility work
- Swimming
- Easy hiking

Aim for low intensity — should be able to hold a conversation easily. Keep heart rate under 60% of max.

## Stretching and Mobility

**Static stretching** (holding a position): Best done AFTER workouts or on rest days. Each stretch held 30-45 seconds. Improves flexibility over time. Doing extensive static stretching before strength training can temporarily reduce power output.

**Dynamic stretching** (moving through ranges): Best done BEFORE workouts as part of warm-up. Examples: leg swings, arm circles, walking lunges, inchworms.

**Foam rolling / self-myofascial release:** Spend 1-2 minutes per major muscle group. Roll slowly, pause on tight spots. Most beneficial post-workout or on rest days.

**Mobility drills:** Joint-specific movements through full ranges of motion. Examples: cat-cow for spine, 90/90 hip rotations, shoulder dislocates with a band.

A 10-15 minute daily mobility routine pays huge dividends in lifting performance and joint longevity.

## DOMS — Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness

That deep ache 24-72 hours after training, especially after new exercises or eccentric work (lowering phase).

**What it is:** Micro-damage to muscle fibers — not lactic acid (a myth).

**It's not a good measure of workout quality.** You don't need to be sore to grow. In fact, persistent severe soreness can indicate over-doing it.

**How to manage DOMS:**
- Light activity (walking, easy bike)
- Stay hydrated
- Eat enough protein
- Sleep well
- Heat or warm baths
- Massage / foam rolling
- Time — it resolves on its own in 2-4 days

**Don't:** Take painkillers regularly (NSAIDs can blunt muscle growth), train through severe soreness on the same muscles.

## Rest Days

You need them. Muscle protein synthesis stays elevated for 24-72 hours after training — this is when growth actually happens, not in the gym.

**Beginners:** 3-4 rest days per week (training 3-4 days)
**Intermediate:** 2-3 rest days per week (training 4-5 days)
**Advanced:** 1-2 rest days per week (training 5-6 days)

Take at least one **complete rest day** per week — no structured exercise, light walking is fine. Your nervous system needs this break too.

## Recognizing Overtraining

**Early signs:**
- Persistent fatigue beyond normal soreness
- Strength gains stall for 2+ weeks
- Disrupted sleep (especially trouble falling asleep)
- Elevated resting heart rate
- Loss of motivation
- Mood changes — irritability, depression

**What to do:**
- Take a deload week (50% of normal volume/intensity)
- If symptoms persist, take 1-2 weeks off completely
- Reassess your program — too much volume? Not enough recovery?
- Check nutrition — are you eating enough?

Overtraining is rare in true beginners but very common in intermediates who chase progress too aggressively.

## Common Injuries and Prevention

### Lower Back Pain

**Causes:** Poor lifting form (rounded back), weak core, sitting all day, tight hip flexors.

**Prevention:**
- Master the hip hinge — practice with a dowel down your back
- Strengthen your core with planks, dead bugs, bird dogs
- Don't ego-lift on deadlifts and squats
- Take frequent breaks from sitting
- Stretch hip flexors daily if you sit a lot

### Knee Pain

**Causes:** Poor squat/lunge mechanics, weak glutes, tight quads, sudden volume increases.

**Prevention:**
- Strengthen glutes with hip thrusts, side-lying leg raises
- Improve ankle mobility — calf stretches, wall ankle drills
- Track knees over toes in squats — don't let them cave
- Don't increase running mileage more than 10% per week

### Shoulder Impingement

**Causes:** Overhead pressing with poor form, weak rotator cuff, tight chest, poor posture.

**Prevention:**
- Strengthen rear delts and rotator cuff (face pulls, external rotations)
- Stretch chest and front delts
- Use neutral grip pressing if straight bar bothers you
- Don't lower the bar to the chest on bench if your shoulders complain — stop short

### Wrist Pain

**Causes:** Poor wrist position during pressing, weak wrists.

**Prevention:**
- Keep wrists stacked over elbows during press
- Train wrist flexion/extension and grip strength
- Wear wrist wraps for heavy work

### Tennis/Golf Elbow

**Causes:** Overuse — gripping too hard, too much pulling, sudden volume jumps.

**Prevention:**
- Vary your grip (overhand, underhand, neutral)
- Add direct forearm work
- Use thicker bars or "fat grips" sparingly
- Reduce volume if symptoms appear early

## When to See a Professional

**See a doctor/physiotherapist if:**
- Pain persists more than 1-2 weeks
- Sharp pain (vs dull ache)
- Numbness, tingling, or weakness
- Joint instability or "giving way"
- Pain worsens with rest or wakes you at night
- Visible swelling, bruising, or deformity

**Don't try to push through real injuries.** A week off now saves months off later. Listen to your body.

## Cold Therapy and Heat

**Cold (ice baths, cold showers):** Reduces inflammation and perceived soreness. **Note:** May actually blunt muscle growth if done within 4 hours of training. Use sparingly or save for off-days during muscle-building phases.

**Heat (warm bath, sauna):** Increases blood flow, helps with chronic tightness. Sauna use 2-3x per week is associated with cardiovascular benefits. Safe to use post-workout.

## Stress and Recovery

Mental stress and physical stress share the same recovery resources. Chronic high stress = poor recovery, even with perfect nutrition and sleep.

**Manage stress through:**
- Meditation or breathwork (5-10 min/day)
- Time outdoors / nature exposure
- Social connection
- Hobbies unrelated to fitness
- Therapy if needed

Cortisol elevated chronically (from stress, lack of sleep, undereating) impairs muscle growth, increases fat storage, and tanks libido.
