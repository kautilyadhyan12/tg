# Workout Programming Principles

## Progressive Overload

Progressive overload is the foundation of all strength and muscle gains. To get stronger, you must gradually increase the demands on your body over time.

**Ways to apply progressive overload:**
1. Add weight to the bar/dumbbell
2. Add more reps at the same weight
3. Add an extra set
4. Reduce rest time between sets
5. Improve form/range of motion
6. Add a more difficult variation

**Beginner approach:** Add 2.5-5 lbs per week to compound lifts (squat, deadlift, bench, row, press). Form should always come first — never sacrifice technique for more weight.

**Intermediate approach:** Add weight every 2-3 weeks. Track sessions in an app or journal. Plateaus are normal — try changing rep ranges or exercise variations.

**Advanced approach:** Periodization — cycle between strength phases (low reps, heavy weight) and hypertrophy phases (higher reps, moderate weight).

## Sets and Reps Guide

Different rep ranges target different adaptations:

- **1-5 reps (heavy):** Maximum strength, neural adaptation. Rest 3-5 minutes between sets.
- **6-12 reps (moderate):** Hypertrophy (muscle growth). The "sweet spot" for size. Rest 60-90 seconds.
- **12-20 reps (light):** Muscular endurance, capillary density. Rest 30-60 seconds.
- **20+ reps:** Cardiovascular endurance, work capacity. Minimal rest.

For most general fitness goals, **3 sets of 8-12 reps** is the gold standard.

## Workout Splits

**Full body (3x/week)** — Best for beginners. Each session hits all major muscle groups. Examples: Monday/Wednesday/Friday.

**Upper/Lower (4x/week)** — Intermediate. Upper body days alternate with lower body days. Good balance of frequency and recovery.

**Push/Pull/Legs (6x/week)** — Advanced. Push (chest/shoulders/triceps), Pull (back/biceps), Legs. Can be done as 3-day or 6-day rotation.

**Body part split (5-6x/week)** — One muscle group per session. Old-school bodybuilding style. Requires high training volume and recovery capacity.

## Frequency

How often should you train each muscle group?

- **Beginners:** 2-3x per week per muscle group. Full body workouts work great.
- **Intermediate:** 2x per week per muscle group for optimal growth.
- **Advanced:** 1-2x per week with higher volume, or 3x per week with lower volume per session.

Research consistently shows that training a muscle 2x per week is superior to 1x for hypertrophy, when total volume is matched.

## Volume and Intensity

**Volume** = sets × reps × weight. Total work done.
**Intensity** = how heavy relative to your max (or how close to failure).

For muscle growth: 10-20 sets per muscle group per week is the sweet spot.
For strength: 10-15 sets per muscle group per week with higher intensity (heavier weights).

Don't increase both volume and intensity at the same time — that's a recipe for injury and burnout.

## Compound vs Isolation

**Compound exercises** involve multiple joints and muscle groups: squat, deadlift, bench press, pull-up, overhead press, row. These should be the foundation of any program.

**Isolation exercises** target a single muscle: bicep curl, tricep extension, lateral raise, leg curl. Use these as accessories to bring up weak points or add volume safely.

A good template: 60-70% compounds, 30-40% isolation.

## Warm-Up and Cool-Down

**Warm-up (5-10 min):**
- 3-5 minutes of light cardio (jumping jacks, jogging in place, jump rope)
- Dynamic stretches: arm circles, leg swings, hip openers
- 1-2 warm-up sets of your first exercise with lighter weight

**Cool-down (5-10 min):**
- Light cardio to bring heart rate down
- Static stretches for the muscles you worked, hold each 30-45 seconds
- Foam rolling for tight areas

Skipping warm-up dramatically increases injury risk. Skipping cool-down impairs recovery.

## Periodization

Periodization means structuring training in cycles with different focuses:

**Linear periodization:** Start with high reps/light weight, gradually shift to low reps/heavy weight over weeks. Good for beginners and intermediates.

**Undulating periodization:** Vary rep ranges within the same week. Monday heavy/low rep, Wednesday moderate, Friday light/high rep. More advanced but very effective.

**Block periodization:** Distinct training blocks of 3-6 weeks each focused on one quality (hypertrophy block → strength block → peaking block). Used by competitive athletes.

## Deloading

Every 4-8 weeks, take a deload week — reduce volume by 40-50% or intensity by 20%. This lets your nervous system recover and prevents overtraining. Signs you need a deload: persistent fatigue, no strength gains for 2+ weeks, joint aches, poor sleep, low motivation.

## Choosing a Program by Goal

**Weight loss:** Full body strength training 3x/week + 2-3 cardio sessions. Focus on compound movements to maximize calorie burn.

**Muscle gain:** Upper/lower or push/pull/legs split. 8-12 rep range. Eat in a slight calorie surplus.

**Strength:** Low reps (3-5), heavy weight, long rest periods (3-5 minutes). Compound lifts only.

**Endurance:** Higher rep ranges (15-20), shorter rest (30-45 seconds), circuit-style training.

**General fitness:** Mix of strength, cardio, and mobility. 3-4 sessions per week.
