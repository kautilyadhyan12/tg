# Exercise Form Guide

## Squat Form

The squat is a foundational lower-body exercise targeting quads, glutes, and hamstrings. Proper form is critical to prevent knee and lower back injury.

**Setup:** Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, toes slightly pointed out (15-30 degrees). Keep chest up and core engaged. Eyes forward.

**Descent:** Initiate by pushing hips back as if sitting in a chair. Bend knees while keeping them tracking over your toes — don't let them cave inward. Lower until thighs are parallel to the floor (or as deep as mobility allows without rounding your back).

**Ascent:** Drive through your heels and mid-foot. Squeeze glutes as you stand. Keep torso angle consistent.

**Common mistakes:** Knees caving inward (valgus), heels lifting, rounded lower back, looking down. Fix by strengthening glutes, improving ankle mobility, and bracing the core.

**Beginners** should start with bodyweight squats or chair squats before adding weight. Aim for 3 sets of 10-15 reps with 60-90 seconds rest.

## Push-Up Form

The push-up trains chest, shoulders, triceps, and core. It's a measuring stick for upper-body strength.

**Setup:** Hands slightly wider than shoulders, fingers spread. Body in straight line from head to heels. Engage core, squeeze glutes.

**Descent:** Lower chest to within an inch of the floor. Elbows should track at about 45 degrees from torso — not flared straight out (90 degrees) which stresses shoulders.

**Ascent:** Push the floor away. Full lockout at the top. Keep body rigid the whole time.

**Common mistakes:** Sagging hips, flared elbows, partial range of motion, head jutting forward.

**Modifications:** Beginners can do incline push-ups (hands on bench or wall) or knee push-ups. Advanced: decline, diamond, archer, one-arm progressions.

## Deadlift Form

The deadlift trains the entire posterior chain — hamstrings, glutes, lower back, traps, lats, forearms.

**Setup:** Stand with feet hip-width apart, barbell over mid-foot. Bend at hips and knees to grip the bar just outside knees. Chest up, back flat (neutral spine), shoulders slightly in front of bar.

**Pull:** Drive through the floor with your legs. Keep the bar close to your body (it should drag up your shins and thighs). Lock out hips and knees simultaneously at the top — stand tall, don't lean back.

**Descent:** Hinge at hips first, then bend knees once the bar passes them. Control the weight back to the floor.

**Common mistakes:** Rounded back (most dangerous mistake), bar drifting away from body, jerking the bar off the floor, hyperextending at lockout.

## Plank Form

The plank is an isometric core exercise that also engages shoulders, glutes, and quads.

**Setup:** Forearms on the floor, elbows directly under shoulders. Feet hip-width apart, toes tucked. Body forms a straight line from head to heels.

**Hold:** Engage core (think: bracing for a punch). Squeeze glutes. Don't let hips sag or pike up. Breathe normally.

**Time goals:** Beginners aim for 20-30 seconds. Intermediate: 60 seconds. Advanced: 2+ minutes with perfect form.

**Common mistakes:** Sagging hips, lifted hips, holding breath, looking up (strains neck).

## Lunge Form

Lunges train each leg independently, helping correct imbalances and build unilateral strength.

**Setup:** Stand tall, hands on hips or holding dumbbells at sides.

**Step:** Take a controlled step forward — roughly 2-3 feet. Lower until both knees form 90-degree angles. Front knee tracks over (not past) the toes. Back knee hovers just above the floor.

**Return:** Push through the front heel to return to standing.

**Common mistakes:** Front knee caving in, leaning forward at the torso, taking too small a step, letting back knee bang the floor.

## Bicep Curl Form

Isolation exercise targeting the biceps brachii.

**Setup:** Stand with feet hip-width, dumbbells at sides, palms facing forward. Keep elbows pinned to your sides.

**Curl:** Bend at the elbow, raising the weight toward your shoulder. Squeeze the biceps at the top. Keep elbows stationary — don't swing your body or let elbows drift forward.

**Lower:** Control the weight down to full extension. The negative (eccentric) is just as important as the lift.

**Common mistakes:** Using momentum, partial range of motion, elbow drift, shrugging shoulders.

## Shoulder Press Form

Compound movement for shoulders (front and lateral deltoids) and triceps.

**Setup:** Sit or stand with dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing forward. Core braced, glutes engaged if standing.

**Press:** Push the weights overhead until arms are fully extended. Bring the dumbbells slightly together at the top (but don't clang them).

**Lower:** Control back to shoulder level. Don't bounce.

**Common mistakes:** Arching the lower back (use a bench with back support if this happens), pressing forward instead of straight up, locking elbows aggressively.
