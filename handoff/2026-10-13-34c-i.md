Drumsticks, wings and lamb chops already had a piece weight from the entry they cite, so nothing changed for them.
- **How the numbers got there:** a script read each row from the loaded `usda_foods` table, so none was typed. `corepack pnpm --filter api exec tsx tools/check-food-sources.ts` then compared all 410 with the downloaded tables: "410 foods checked (usda-sr 333, usda-fndds 72, uk-cofid 5): every number matches its table entry".
- **Worst thing, first test** (`nutrition.foods.diet.unit.test.ts`): a food that holds meat, fish, egg or milk labelled vegetarian or vegan. Each of the 333 SR Legacy foods' labels is held to USDA's OWN description and food group, kept in `test/fixtures/usda-sr-food-groups.json`; the checker tool writes that file (`--write-groups`) and fails when it differs from the download. Seven plant foods whose USDA words read as animal (coconut "meat", peanut "butter", almond "milk") are excused by id, each with its reason. One break by hand, put back: anchovies and ricotta labelled vegan, both named RED. **The 72 survey foods have no food group in USDA's files; no new food is one of them**, and 34c-ii, which adds dishes, should check theirs against the survey release's ingredient file (`input_food.csv`).
- **A name still means the food it meant:** 3,058 names (every food's name, its words alone and in pairs, every other name) were asked of the old list and the new. Four answer differently, all meant: tangerine and mandarin, singular and plural. The fixed photo plates changed in ten rows, all meant and pinned again: four shrimp rows by the piece; pumpkin, lemon, sesame seeds, raw broccoli and coconut milk are our list's where they were USDA's or an estimate.
