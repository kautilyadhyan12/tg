# 2026-10-13 · 34c-ii (feature folder 2): the traditional dishes of the US, Canada, Europe, Australia and New Zealand (branch `more-foods-dishes`)

- **Why this job:** items 31, 32b, 33 and 34b are phone screens and wait for feature folder 1's item 12 (sign-in). 34c-ii needs no phone. **Ordinary** (no sign-in, money, other people's data or outside reply). Kd: *"go"*. **Built at Opus 5.5 medium** (his `/model` line before "go"; xhigh was recommended and said). No database change, no package, no model call, no cost.
- **Built, no screen:** the food list grew from 409 to 510 (`modules/nutrition/foods.ts`): 33 American dishes, 41 European, 27 British, Irish, Australian and New Zealand. 66 are USDA's survey foods, 6 SR Legacy's, 29 the UK table's (the British dishes USDA lacks, and moussaka, goulash, coq au vin, risotto). Other names people type were added (meat loaf, BLT, dolmades, latkes, Vegemite, leg of lamb).
- **How the numbers got there:** a script read each row from the downloaded tables, so none was typed. `corepack pnpm --filter api exec tsx tools/check-food-sources.ts`: "510 foods checked (usda-sr 338, usda-fndds 138, uk-cofid 34): every number matches its table entry".
- **Worst thing, first test** (`nutrition.foods.diet.unit.test.ts`): a dish that holds meat, fish, egg or milk labelled vegetarian or vegan. Every survey food the list cites, the 72 older ones too, is held to USDA's OWN ingredient list (the survey release's `input_food.csv`, read down through an ingredient that is itself a survey food), each ingredient with its SR Legacy food group, kept in `test/fixtures/usda-fndds-ingredients.json`; a UK-table dish to that table's own name and note (`uk-cofid-names.json`). The checker tool writes both (`--write-groups`) and fails when either differs from the download. An ingredient no food group and no word settles (53 of them, in dishes labelled for somebody who eats no meat) is on a list in the test with what reading it found, and a new one fails the test until it is read. One break by hand, put back: clam chowder labelled vegetarian and pumpkin soup vegan, both named RED.
- **What that test cannot see:** a dish USDA makes from one bought food (a frozen pierogi, a restaurant tamale) and every UK-table dish show no ingredients. Their labels are the terminal's reading (`rulings/2026-10-13-34c-ii.md`), pinned in `nutrition.foods.unit.test.ts`.
- **A name still means the food it meant:** 1,876 names (every food's name, its words alone and in pairs, every other name, and 89 plain words) were asked of the old list and the new: none that named a food now names another. The fixed photo plates changed in one row, meant and pinned again: a photo's "chicken salad" is our "Chicken salad" where it was USDA's "Chicken salad spread", the same entry and the same numbers.
- **Verified here** on `aihg_b`: the 16 food and USDA test files (`test:local test/nutrition test/usda`), 528 of 528; api typecheck and lint clean (remembering runs).
- **Cost:** nothing reads a list of people. The list is searched in memory, 510 rows where it was 409 (not timed).
- **Left out, and why:** the dishes neither table holds (poutine, butter tarts, tourtière, lamingtons, Anzac biscuits, a meat pie, schnitzel, carbonara) wait for a country's table whose licence is read and that Kd has said yes to; Canada, Australia and New Zealand got no dish of their own beyond pavlova, roast lamb, pumpkin soup and yeast extract spread. Rice pilaf: USDA makes it from a boxed mix whose stock is not said.
