# 2026-11-17 · 50c-ii, is this body possible, and steady points (exercise folder 1; branch `body-possible-steady`)

- **Why:** the new engine believed every point the model gave. On Kd's videos the model lifts the squat's near ankle 10 cm while she stands (95% sure), and on both bench videos it folds the skeleton onto his head, draws his arms on the bench and finds his reflection. Spec Part 2 §12.2 stages 2 and 3.
- **Built:** `PossibleBody` and `SteadyPoints` in `packages/movement/src/body` (pure; the points, their bones and each point's parent towards the trunk; the body's size on the picture from its 3D points). `body-check`: the check on every kept video, each fitted number set from five people and tried on the sixth, half the frames a second, pictures of every refused frame. A stand-in follower (`scripts/follow.ts`) until 52d.
- **The numbers set by rule:** bone allowance 0.25 of the trunk (the smallest at which no one-person video loses more than 1 in 200 of its sure joints); steady 0.5 Hz at rest +10 Hz a m/s on the picture, 0.05 Hz +10 in 3D (the steadiest that keep a moving joint within 1 cm more than its shake). Five of six folds set the same. Without the crunch the others set 0.15 and the crunch loses 6.1%: a new kind of movement can need it set again.
- **From outside, not fitted:** 20 m/s (martial arts studies: a kick's foot 12 to 18 m/s, a punch 6 to 11); an even chance of being seen; 0.2 s to settle (two more looks at 8 a second); 10 cm before left and right can swap (ten times a joint's shake).
- **On Kd's six videos (big joints):** used 48% to 73%; hidden 23% to 45% (the far side, side on); on the one-person videos no bone, jump or swap but the crunch's arm behind the head (0.1%); bench 2.4% and 0.8% bone, 1.5% jump, 0.6% swap. Half the frames a second: 0 to 0.2% answered differently on one-person videos, 2.9% on bench. 282 microseconds a frame on the laptop.
- **Found while building:** a trunk-share allowance looked unfair to long legs; judging each bone by its own length needed 0.45 and let most bench faults through, and the model's error does not grow with bone length (crunch's short upper arms wander most), so the trunk share stays. "All of a point's bones broken" let a garbage arm vouch for itself (the garbage wrist used in 30 to 51% of frames): replaced by the bone towards the trunk (under 20%). A jump that stayed was used one frame later: now compared with where it jumped to first. A wrist with a hidden elbow was checked by nothing: now by its other bones.
- **What it cannot catch:** the squat's lifted ankle at 2.8 to 3.8 s passes in all 30 frames (52a's two signs must stop it); at 8 frames a second only a move over 2.5 m is a jump; after a jump, a point already back in the right place waits 0.2 s; the follower may follow the reflection once the man is lost (52d).
