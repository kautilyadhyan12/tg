# Nutrition for Fitness

## Calorie Basics

Your body uses calories for everything — breathing, thinking, moving, recovering from workouts. Whether you gain, lose, or maintain weight depends on calorie balance.

**Calories in vs calories out:**
- **Surplus** (eating more than you burn): weight gain
- **Deficit** (eating less than you burn): weight loss
- **Maintenance** (eating same as you burn): no change

**TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)** = your maintenance calories. It depends on:
- BMR (basal metabolic rate) — calories burned at rest
- Activity level — exercise, daily movement, NEAT
- Thermic effect of food — calories burned digesting

A rough estimate for adults:
- **Sedentary:** bodyweight (kg) × 25-28
- **Moderately active:** bodyweight × 30-33
- **Very active:** bodyweight × 35-40

For weight loss: subtract 300-500 calories from TDEE for a sustainable rate (0.5-1 lb per week).
For muscle gain: add 200-400 calories above TDEE.

## Macronutrients

**Protein (4 cal/g):** Builds and repairs muscle. Essential for anyone training. 
- General fitness: 0.8-1.0g per kg bodyweight
- Muscle gain: 1.6-2.2g per kg bodyweight
- Cutting (fat loss): 2.0-2.4g per kg bodyweight (preserves muscle in a deficit)

Best sources: chicken, fish, eggs, lean beef, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, lentils, whey protein.

**Carbohydrates (4 cal/g):** Primary fuel for high-intensity training and brain function.
- Active individuals: 3-5g per kg bodyweight
- Endurance athletes: 5-8g per kg
- Cutting: lower carbs to 2-3g per kg

Best sources: oats, rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, fruit, whole grain bread, quinoa, beans.

**Fats (9 cal/g):** Hormone production, vitamin absorption, joint health.
- Minimum: 0.5g per kg bodyweight (don't go below this for hormone health)
- Typical: 0.7-1.0g per kg

Best sources: olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish (salmon, sardines), eggs, whole dairy.

## Protein Timing

Spread protein across 4-5 meals/day, ~0.4g per kg per meal. The body can only use ~25-40g of protein for muscle protein synthesis per meal, so timing matters.

**Pre-workout:** 20-40g protein 1-2 hours before training. Helps prevent muscle breakdown.

**Post-workout:** 20-40g protein within 1-2 hours after training. The "anabolic window" is actually 4-6 hours wide, not 30 minutes, so don't stress about racing home.

**Before bed:** Slow-digesting protein like cottage cheese or casein helps overnight recovery.

## Hydration

Water is critical — even 2% dehydration impairs strength and endurance significantly.

**Daily target:** 35ml per kg bodyweight (about 2.5-3.5L for most adults).
**Workout days:** Add 500-750ml extra per hour of training.
**Signs of dehydration:** Dark yellow urine, headache, fatigue, dizziness, cramping.

Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) matter for workouts lasting over 60 minutes or in hot conditions.

## Pre-Workout Nutrition

**2-3 hours before:** Balanced meal with carbs + protein + some fat. Example: chicken with rice and veggies.

**30-60 minutes before:** Lighter snack, easy to digest. Example: banana with a scoop of whey, or oatmeal.

**Right before:** Optional — black coffee, BCAAs, or nothing if eating earlier.

Avoid: heavy fats, very high fiber, lots of dairy (if sensitive) close to training.

## Post-Workout Nutrition

Goal: replenish glycogen + provide amino acids for repair.

**Within 1-2 hours:**
- 20-40g protein
- 30-60g carbs (more if it was a long/intense session)

Examples:
- Protein shake + banana
- Chicken + sweet potato + vegetables
- Greek yogurt + berries + granola
- Tuna sandwich on whole grain bread

## Meal Examples by Goal

### Weight Loss (1500-1800 cal/day)
- **Breakfast:** 3 egg omelette with spinach + 1 slice whole grain toast (~350 cal)
- **Lunch:** Grilled chicken salad with olive oil dressing (~450 cal)
- **Snack:** Greek yogurt with berries (~150 cal)
- **Dinner:** Salmon, quinoa, roasted vegetables (~500 cal)
- **Total protein:** ~130g

### Muscle Gain (2800-3200 cal/day)
- **Breakfast:** Oats, banana, peanut butter, whey shake (~700 cal)
- **Mid-morning:** Cottage cheese with granola (~400 cal)
- **Lunch:** Chicken, rice, vegetables, avocado (~800 cal)
- **Pre-workout:** Greek yogurt + honey + nuts (~400 cal)
- **Post-workout:** Whey shake + banana (~350 cal)
- **Dinner:** Lean beef, sweet potato, vegetables (~700 cal)
- **Total protein:** ~180g

### Maintenance / General Fitness (2200-2500 cal/day)
- **Breakfast:** Greek yogurt, granola, berries (~400 cal)
- **Lunch:** Turkey sandwich with vegetables (~500 cal)
- **Snack:** Apple with almond butter (~250 cal)
- **Dinner:** Chicken stir-fry with rice (~650 cal)
- **Evening:** Casein protein or cottage cheese (~200 cal)
- **Total protein:** ~150g

## Foods to Prioritize

**Lean proteins:** Chicken breast, turkey, lean beef, fish (salmon, tuna, cod), eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, tempeh, legumes.

**Complex carbs:** Oats, brown rice, quinoa, sweet potato, whole grain bread/pasta, fruits, vegetables.

**Healthy fats:** Avocado, olive oil, nuts, seeds, fatty fish, eggs.

**Micronutrients:** Leafy greens (spinach, kale), berries, cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower), bell peppers.

## Foods to Minimize

- Ultra-processed foods (chips, cookies, instant noodles)
- Sugary drinks (soda, fruit juice, energy drinks)
- Excessive alcohol (impairs recovery and lowers testosterone)
- Trans fats (anything with "partially hydrogenated" on the label)
- Refined carbs in large quantities

You don't have to eliminate these — the 80/20 rule works well: eat clean 80% of the time, enjoy treats 20% of the time.

## Supplements (Evidence-Based)

**Worth it:**
- **Creatine monohydrate** (5g/day) — best-studied supplement, modest strength gains, very safe
- **Whey protein** — convenient way to hit protein targets
- **Vitamin D** (1000-2000 IU) — most people are deficient, important for muscle and bone health
- **Caffeine** (100-300mg pre-workout) — improves performance, focus, endurance
- **Fish oil** (1-3g EPA/DHA) — joint health, inflammation

**Probably not worth it for most people:**
- BCAAs (just eat protein)
- Pre-workouts (caffeine + hype)
- "Test boosters" (gimmicky)
- Fat burners (mostly caffeine)

## Special Diets

**Vegetarian/Vegan:** Totally compatible with fitness. Focus on complete proteins (combine grains + legumes), use plant-based protein powders, supplement B12 and possibly creatine.

**Keto:** Very low carb (under 50g/day), high fat. Can work for fat loss but limits high-intensity performance. Not optimal for muscle gain.

**Intermittent fasting:** Eating window of 8 hours, fasting 16 hours. Works for some people as a calorie-control tool. Doesn't have magical benefits beyond that.

**Mediterranean diet:** High in fish, olive oil, vegetables, whole grains. One of the most researched and healthiest dietary patterns.
