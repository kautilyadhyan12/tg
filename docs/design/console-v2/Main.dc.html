The rules every page follows

The same rules the design schools teach and the big gym and business apps use. Nothing is taken away: every button keeps what it does. Members & staff keeps its look, and every other page now looks and works like it.

Fourteen rules

Each one fixes something you can see on today's pages.

1

A person gets a whole page

Press a name and their own page opens, full width: who they are on the left, their memberships and money in the middle. No more narrow strip stuck in the corner.

Jakob's law · figure and ground

2

Look where the eye lands first

Top left says who and what state they are in (Active, Payment due). Top right holds the main button. The rest reads top to bottom.

F-pattern reading · visual hierarchy

3

What belongs together sits together

Small gaps inside a group, big gaps between groups: 8 between a label and its box, 20 between boxes, 32 between groups.

Gestalt: proximity and common region

4

One orange button per box

Orange is the next step. A second important action is pale orange; everything else is outlined, so the eye is never pulled two ways.

Isolation effect (Von Restorff)

5

Red is last, and kept apart

Cancel, Remove and Delete sit away from the main button and always ask first, naming who changes and what happens.

Fitts's law · error prevention (Norman)

6

Forms never push the page around

A short form opens under its button, a longer one slides in from the right, a question opens in the middle, a big setup gets its own page with steps.

Consistency and standards (Nielsen)

7

Fewer choices at once

The menu is grouped into five short lists. Long pages get tabs or a side list, so you only see the part you need.

Hick's law · Miller's law (chunks)

8

Summary first, detail on a press

A list shows one line a thing with its status. Press it to see the rest. Long explanations move into a small hint.

Progressive disclosure

9

Say it once

One list for one thing: never the same five people twice on a page, never the same box on two pages.

Signal-to-noise · one source of truth

10

Five text sizes, three greys

32 page title, 18 box title, 15 words, 13 small print, and 40 for the number that matters. Text always readable: 4.5 to 1 or more.

Typographic scale · WCAG contrast

11

Status is a word in a colour

Green good, amber look at this, red a problem, always with the word beside it: Active, Payment due, Overdue.

Recognition over recall · WCAG 1.4.1

12

Big enough for a thumb

On a phone every button is at least 44 tall, quick forms rise from the bottom, and the main button stays at the bottom of the screen.

Fitts's law · WCAG 2.5.8

13

Words follow the kind of business

These designs show a gym. When they are built, every word that names the place, its people or its staff comes from the kind of business the owner picked, as the app already does: nothing is written into a page. A yoga studio reads “studio” and “members”; a Pilates studio reads “clients” and “instructors”. The name the owner typed, like Northside Strength, is used wherever the designs show it.

14

It looks new, it works the same

Building these designs changes where things sit, their size, colour and grouping, and where a box opens. It never changes what a button does, who may do what, what is saved, what a question asks, or the rules behind any of it. Nothing is added and nothing is taken away.

Stays exactly the sameEvery button and what it does · every field · every message and question · who can see and do what · every rule and number the app works out
ChangesWhere things sit · sizes, colours and spacing · how boxes are grouped · whether a form opens under its button, from the right, in the middle or on its own page
Shortcuts onlyThe one-press picks above Members (Payment due, Not in the app…) press the same choice in Filter for you; Filter stays with every choice it has.

Kd, 18 October 2026: “not changing functionality and feature”

Where things open

Four kinds, used the same way on every page. Nothing opens inside a list and pushes it down any more.

Under the button

One to three questions: record a payment, add a tag, pick a day. It floats; nothing moves.

From the right

A form of four or more fields: edit details, add a membership. The page stays in view behind it. The panel scrolls as one page; only Save stays at its foot, with a line when something is not saved yet.

In the middle

A question before a change to people or money: cancel, remove, give money back. It names who changes.

A page of its own

A big setup: a membership type, a challenge, a new gym. Numbered steps, and a summary that fills in as you go.

Spacing that groups

The blue bands show the gaps. The eye reads small gaps as "together".

Why five groups

Sixteen names in one column take a long look to read. Five short groups are read at a glance, and the group says what the page is for: what happens today at the desk, the people, the money, the community in the app, and running the business.

What does not change

The same pages, the same names, shown to the same people as today. Members are still "Clients" for a studio or a trainer. On a phone the bottom bar keeps Overview, Members, Attendance, Classes and More.

What is new

Group names, your name and Sign out always visible at the bottom (today they drop off the screen on a laptop), and the Light · Dark · Auto switch from September's drawings.
